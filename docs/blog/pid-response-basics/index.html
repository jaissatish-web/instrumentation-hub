Learn PID by changing one parameter at a time

Watch setpoint tracking, output limits and disturbance recovery in a teaching simulation.

✍️ Satish Kumar Jaiswal|📅 2026-09-23|🕐 5 min read
🛠 PID Loop Simulator →

A PID controller uses the difference between setpoint and measured process value to adjust an output. The simulator lets you compare these signals on one chart. It uses a simple first-order process so the effect of changing a parameter is visible without connecting to real equipment.

Begin with proportional action

Set integral and derivative gains to zero. Increase proportional gain gradually and compare the speed of response and the remaining error. For this process model, proportional-only control generally needs a persistent error to maintain a nonzero output.

Add integral action

Integral action accumulates error and can remove steady offset when the output has enough authority. An aggressive integral setting may produce overshoot or poor recovery. The simulator limits output to 0–100% and uses conditional integration to reduce windup while saturated.

Understand derivative and conventions

This implementation applies derivative action to the measured value. Its parallel-form Ki is in inverse seconds and Kd in seconds. A plant controller may instead use integral time, proportional band or another algorithm. Numbers cannot be copied between those conventions without conversion.

Try a comparison
Keep the process time constant at 10 s and gain at 1. Use Kp = 2, Ki = 0 and Kd = 0, then change Ki to 0.1. Compare the final error and the response to the disturbance introduced at 100 s.

Read all three traces

The process-value trace alone can hide saturation. Compare it with controller output and setpoint. If the required steady output lies beyond 0–100%, integral action cannot make the process reach the target.

This is a fixed-step educational simulation with no dead time, measurement noise, valve friction or interlocks. It does not calculate safe plant tuning settings. Use it to understand relationships before reviewing an approved tuning method for a real process.
